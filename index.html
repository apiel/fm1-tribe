TRIBE

Groovebox firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1. Ten tracks, and any engine on any track: kicks, the 909 and 808 voices, the 303. Sixteen clips per track, scatter effects, two master effects every track can send to, and four master knobs you assign to the volume, a DJ filter, the effects or the tempo.

TRIBE is a beta, built on X0X and Felucca. It installs and uninstalls the way they do, and the installer can put M-VAVE's own firmware back. Installing is at your own risk.

1Install

Try it first: the TRIBE emulator runs the same code in the browser, with sound, played with the mouse, a touch screen or the keyboard.

Install: open the web installer in Chrome or Edge, connect the FM-1 with a USB data cable and press Install. Don't unplug it while it's writing; when it finishes, the FM-1 restarts into TRIBE.

Go back: "Back to the stock firmware", at the bottom of the same page, puts M-VAVE's firmware back (download the FM-1 V15 file from the link there, choose it, press the button). M-VAVE's own updater, M-UPGRADE, works too.

Installing other firmware is at your own risk. If the FM-1 ever won't start, see Recovering an FM-1 that won't start.

Your saved clips stay in the FM-1's memory when you install a newer version. The settings (tempo, levels, effects, knob setup) go back to their defaults after an install that changes how they are stored; the what's-new page says when that happens.

Install from the command line

Download the firmware file (for example tribe-0.1-beta.fwsc) from the releases page, get the TRIBE source from GitHub, install Python 3 with pip3 install mido python-rtmidi, and run:

python3 tools/fm1_install.py tribe-0.1-beta.fwsc

tools/flash.sh does the same for a build you made yourself (see BUILDING.md).

2Getting started

TRIBE starts with a small beat already in it, so there is something to hear straight away.

  1. Press PLAY. Ten tracks are lined up, and several already have a pattern.
  2. Press a black key to pick a track. The black keys are the tracks, 1 to 10, left to right. The screen's header names the track and its engine.
  3. The white keys are the sixteen steps of the track. Press one to switch a step on, press it again to switch it off. Hold an "on" step to edit it: the three or four knobs become its note, velocity, probability (and length and slide on an ACID).
  4. Press EDIT, ENV or FX to shape the track's sound with the four knobs.
  5. Hold the eleventh black key (MUTE) and tap a track key to mute or unmute that track.
  6. Press HOME for the clips: sixty-four per track, in banks of sixteen. Press HOME again for the next bank. Press a white key to launch one. It switches at the end of the pattern; press it again to switch at once.
  7. Press SEL for the mix: each track's volume, its two sends and engine.
  8. Press SAVE when you like what you hear.

4Tracks and engines

There are ten tracks. Each plays one engine, which belongs to the clip, so changing clips can change the engine too. Choose it on the SEL page, with KNOB 4.

EngineWhat it is
NONEAn empty track.
KICK WA kick built on a waveform: frequency, FM, folding, harmonics.
KICK PA kick with a morphing oscillator and an FM pair.
909 BD, SD, LT, MT, HT, RS, CP, CH, OH, CR, RDThe 909 voices: kick, snare, three toms, rim, clap, closed and open hat, crash, ride.
808 BD, SD, LT, MT, HT, RS, CP, CB, CY, OH, CHThe 808 voices: kick, snare, three toms, rim, clap, cowbell, cymbal, open and closed hat.
ACIDThe bass synth: the zicCycle 303, with accent, slide, a colour and a pitch envelope, and its LFO.
FM4A mono 4-operator FM synth laid out like the Digitone's FM: operators C, A and B1/B2, eight algorithms with two outputs (X and Y, mixed by MIX), HARM and DTUN, two envelopes with end levels, a filter and an overdrive.
NOISEA mono noise synth: white, pink, brown or dust (sparse clicks) through a drive (soft, hard or fold) and a filter (LP, HP, BP or notch), with key tracking, a built-in modulation (sine, random, drift or gate), an envelope, a bit and sample-rate crusher, and a comb that rings at the note.

Every drum engine is pitched: the note of a step changes its tune, which is what makes the kicks useful. A synth step has a note, a velocity, a length and a slide.

A voice exists once. Each 909 and 808 voice can be on one track at a time (a second track that picks a voice already in use shows BUSY in the header and stays silent), there are two ACIDs, two LEADs and three kicks (KICK W and KICK P share them).

5The sequencer

Press SEQ. The sixteen white keys are the steps of the selected track, with the step the track is playing lit. Press a key to switch a step on or off. Hold an "on" step (about a third of a second) and it stays selected: the knobs become that step's.

  • Drums: NOTE, VELOCITY, PROBABILITY (the chance it plays, 0–100%).
  • ACID: NOTE, VELOCITY, LENGTH (in quarter steps), SLIDE (the note glides into the next).

With no step selected the knobs are greyed and do nothing. A track can be 16, 32 or 64 steps; OCT- and OCT+ page through them, sixteen at a time.

Hold SEQ (about half a second) for the sequencer's settings of the clip, saved with it. Let go to go back to the steps.

SettingValues
LENGTH16, 32 or 64 steps
RATE1/16, 1/8, 1/32, and the triplets 1/16T, 1/8T, 1/32T
DIRECTIONforward, reverse, ping-pong, random
SWING0–100%: the second step of each pair is late

Each track runs on its own clock, so a 1/32 hat can play over a 1/8 bass. A queued clip takes over when the track's pattern ends, in whatever direction it plays.

6Sound pages and the LFO

FX, ENV, LFO and EDIT show the parameters of the selected track's engine in four groups. The four knobs edit the page; the name and value are under each knob. When a group has more than four parameters there are dots in the strip and OCT-/OCT+ (or pressing the button again) flip pages.

What each engine has is listed in section 14. Parameters belong to the clip: switch clips and the sound switches with it.

LFO (the synths only: ACID, LEAD, FM4 and NOISE, since a drum hit is too short for one) is a modulation of one of the engine's parameters. There is one per clip, saved with it.

Control
SHAPEsine, triangle, saw, square, random
RATEcontinuous, one step a click. Synced to the tempo: from 1/64 to 8 bars. Free: from 0.02 Hz (a cycle every 50 s) to 100 Hz; the slow part (under 1 Hz) takes the first third of the knob, so it is coarser there. A change glides, over about 30 ms, so turning it never jumps
DEPTH0 is off; at full the parameter moves half its range each way
TARGETthe parameter it moves
SYNCon: tempo-synced; off: free
RETRIGrestart on every note (with random: a new value on every note)

7Clips

Press HOME. Each track has sixty-four clips, in four banks of sixteen; a clip holds everything of the track: pattern, engine, sound, LFO and sequencer settings. The white keys show one bank at a time: OCT- and OCT+ move between the banks, and so does pressing HOME again (round again after the fourth; the dots at the right of the screen show which one). The grid shows ten rows (the tracks) of the bank's sixteen clips; a filled square is a clip with notes in it, the playing one is lit.

The white keys launch a clip of the selected track:

  • The first press queues it (it blinks) and it takes over at the end of the pattern. It is launched when you let go of the key.
  • Press the same key again while it is queued to switch at once.
  • Hold a clip key for about half a second to open its clip menu. KNOB 1 picks a row: SAVE copies the clip the track is playing, with its unsaved changes, into this one; DELETE empties this one; EXIT closes the menu. Press SAVE to confirm. Any other button closes the menu without doing anything.

KNOB 1 on this page is the track's level. KNOB 4 is the track's song setting:

  • OFF: a clip plays on its own.
  • LOOP: the track plays its clips one after another, from the clip it is on, each for one pattern. The first empty clip ends the run, and the run starts again from its first clip.
  • ONE SHOT: the same, but after the run's last clip the track goes on to the empty clip and stays silent there.

Empty clips are how you end a run, so delete a clip (or leave it empty) where a song should stop.

Changed and not saved? Only the clip a track is playing keeps its edits. When that track launches another clip, the unsaved edits are lost, and the screen says so. Save first (SAVE, then SAVE again, or the pick) to keep an edit. A song moves on by itself: save a clip before a song plays on from it.

8Scatter effects

While it plays, the second row of keys on a sound page (FX, ENV, LFO, EDIT), on SEL or on GLO is not for notes but for scatter effects, held for as long as you press. The first eight act on the selected track, the last eight on the whole mix:

Keys 1–8 (the track)Keys 9–16 (the mix)
1/32, 1/16, 1/8: a loop of that length1/32, 1/16, 1/8: a loop of that length
REV: reverseREV: reverse
GATE: chopGATE: chop
CRSH: bit crusherCRSH: bit crusher
DIST: distortionLPF: a low-pass filter
RING: ring modulatorHPF: a high-pass filter

One effect at a time per scope. Stopped, the same keys audition the track's notes.

9Keys, arpeggiator and recording

ARP turns the whole keyboard into a keyboard for the selected track, from F3 to G5; OCT- and OCT+ move it up and down. The first knob chooses a mode:

Mode
KEYSPlain keyboard.
UP, DOWN, UPDN, RANDThe held keys arpeggiate (with PLAY). RATE (1/16, 1/8, 1/4), GATE and OCT (octaves) are the other knobs.
TRSPTransposition: a key is a number of semitones from middle C (the dot). The track's sequence plays transposed until the next key. Leaving TRSP puts it back.

REC gives the keyboard to the selected track too, and while the track plays it records: a note goes into the nearest step of the track's pattern (a drum hit, or an ACID note whose length is how long you held the key). Stopped, REC just plays.

Capture keeps what you play, even when you are not recording. While the track plays, the keys on ARP and REC are kept: the last 32 notes of the track, each at its nearest step, with the length you held it (a synth; a drum keeps its own). Nothing is written into the clip yet. Press SAVE, then REC: the capture goes into the clip on screen and that clip is saved. Keys played while the track is stopped are not kept, and a clip launch clears the capture.

10The mix: SEL

SEL is the page of what every track has. Each track is a column: the wide bar is its live level (after the volume and the mute), the white tick the volume, and the two thin bars its sends. Black keys pick the track. The white keys work as on the other sound pages: they play the track when it is stopped, and are the scatter keys while it plays.

Knob
VOLUMEthe track's level
SEND 1how much goes to FX 1
SEND 2how much goes to FX 2
ENGINEwhich engine the clip on this track plays

The sends start closed. FX 1 and FX 2 are two master effects (chosen in GLO, next section): raise a track's send to put it in the echo or the room. Sends are after the volume and the mute.

Each of the two master effects is one of:

AMOUNT is how much of the effect returns to the mix. The reverb and the delay have one buffer each, so only one of the two slots can be a reverb and only one a delay. CHORUS, FLANGER and COMB share one short line, so only one of the two slots can be one of those.

The four knobs

MASTER (a pot) and SELECT, PRESETS and ALGORITHM (encoders) each do one of:

VOLUME · DJ FILTER · DJ RESO · FX1 AMOUNT · FX2 AMOUNT · BPM

Choose in GLO with KNOB 2 on the knob's row. A knob and the GLO row of the same name are one value: turn either. BPM on an encoder moves one BPM a click; on the pot, 30 to 300.

The factory setup: MASTER volume, SELECT the DJ filter, PRESETS FX 1's amount, ALGORITHM FX 2's. The DJ filter acts on the whole mix.

The compressor

The master compressor is always on, then a limiter. GLO has its threshold, ratio, attack, release, makeup and mix. The defaults are a gentle glue: -12 dB, 2:1, 8 ms, 110 ms, +2 dB.

12Saving

Nothing is saved on its own. The SAVE light glows when something has changed.

Press SAVE and a pick opens on the screen. Every other button goes dark, SAVE glows at half, and what can be saved blinks:

  • the track keys whose clip on screen has changed,
  • GLO, if the global settings have changed.

Press a blinking track key to save that clip, or GLO to save the settings. REC saves the track's capture (section 9) into its clip on screen; REC blinks when there is one. Press SAVE again to save everything that changed, including clips that are not on screen. Any other button or key cancels. With nothing to save, SAVE only says so.

The setting the MASTER knob is doing is never saved (its volume, if it is the volume): the pot is a position, and TRIBE reads it when it starts. Everything else on GLO is saved, the volume included when MASTER does something else.

Clips are saved to the FM-1's flash, one at a time, only the ones that changed. If the power is cut in the middle of a save, the previous version of the clip is still there.

13Troubleshooting

Update mode: hold OCT- and OCT+ together for five seconds. A countdown appears; let go before it ends to cancel.

Calibration: if a button or knob does the wrong thing, hold OCT- and OCT+ while you switch the FM-1 on. The screen then asks you to press each button and turn each knob in turn.

Questions and bugs: join the fm1-tribe Discord or open an issue.

It's crackling or cutting out: look at CPU in GLO while everything plays. Ten tracks of ACIDs and kicks is a lot for the FM-1; turn tracks to NONE or to simpler engines if the peak reaches 100%.

After a crash: TRIBE shows a red CRASH screen and restarts by itself a few seconds later. The next start shows RESTARTED AFTER A CRASH and an address (PC ...) for a moment. If you report the problem, include that address and what you were doing.

Safe mode: if TRIBE crashes twice in a row while starting, it starts in SAFE MODE instead. There's no sound, but USB works, so the web installer can reach it: reinstall TRIBE, or put the stock firmware back from the same page. Press PLAY to try starting TRIBE normally again. If even safe mode fails twice, the FM-1 drops into the chip's own update mode, and only the rescue below can reach it.

Recovering an FM-1 that won't start

If the FM-1 is in the chip's own update mode (a blank screen after crashes), neither the web installer nor M-VAVE's updater can see it, but a script can put M-VAVE's firmware back, on a Mac, with the USB cable you already have:

Open Terminal, paste this line and press Return:

bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apiel/fm1-tribe/main/tools/fm1_rescue.sh)"
  1. If a window asks to install the command line developer tools, click Install. When it has finished, paste the line again.
  2. If it asks for a password, type your Mac password (nothing shows as you type) and press Return.
  3. When it says it's waiting, plug the FM-1 in and switch it on. It crashes and goes blank, and the script catches it. It checks the chip, saves a backup of the FM-1's memory in the fm1-rescue folder in your home folder, and asks before it writes anything.
  4. Type yes and press Return. Don't unplug anything until it says it's restarting the FM-1. The FM-1 then starts into its original firmware, and you can install TRIBE again from there.

The script downloads M-VAVE's FM-1 V15 firmware and JieLi's flash loader, and checks both against known fingerprints. It writes only the firmware area of the memory, never the bootloader or the FM-1's own settings, and checks everything it writes. Running it again is always safe. If it fails, copy everything in the Terminal window and open an issue. If the FM-1 never shows up at all, Felucca's FM-1-transporter (a small RP2040 board wired to the USB lines) can still reach it.
